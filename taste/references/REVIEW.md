# The critique recipe

You're being asked to judge existing work — yours, someone else's, or a comparison
between a few options. The same grounding from SKILL.md applies (job, audience,
exemplar) — you can't tell whether something succeeds without knowing what it was
supposed to do, for whom, and what "good" looks like in this specific context. What's
different in critique mode is the shape of what you hand back.

## Lead with the verdict

Say, in one or two sentences, whether this works and roughly how well — before any
detail. The reader (or you, reading your own draft) should know where this is going
before the first specific issue shows up. Don't make someone read five paragraphs of
observations to find out whether you thought it was good.

## Then the few things that actually matter

Not every issue you noticed — the ones that materially change whether this succeeds
at its job. A useful test: if you could only tell the author three things, which
three would you pick? Everything below that line either doesn't make the cut or gets
folded into a much shorter mention.

This is a deliberate departure from exhaustive review. A giant scored rubric or a list
of every small nit feels thorough but usually isn't more useful — it forces the reader
to figure out for themselves which of the twenty comments actually matters, which is
the exact job you were supposed to do for them. Rank by impact the same way you would
in create mode, then only surface what's above the line.

Exceptions: if the user explicitly asked for exhaustive/nitpick-level review ("go
through this line by line," "catch every typo"), that's a coverage task — give them
coverage, the same exception as in SKILL.md's activation section.

## Then the fix

For each thing that matters, say what to actually do about it — not just what's
wrong. "The opening buries the recommendation" is an observation. "Move the
recommendation to the first paragraph, cut the background to two sentences" is
something the author can act on immediately. Prefer the second.

## What to actually look for

Pull from the same failure modes SKILL.md describes for create mode, since a critique
is really asking "did the create-mode discipline happen here?":

- **Was there a real exemplar, and does the work actually reflect it** — or does it
  read like it was built from memory/genre convention when a real reference existed?
- **Is attention proportional to importance**, or does everything get the same
  treatment regardless of how much it matters?
- **Is every specific detail grounded**, or are there plausible-sounding facts that
  nobody could actually verify?
- **Does it commit**, or does it hedge across a menu of options where a decision was
  actually wanted?
- **Is the finish level right for the stakes** — under-baked for something that
  matters, or over-polished ceremony around something that didn't need it?
- **Is there template residue** — sections or elements present because that's the
  shape things like this usually take, not because this one needs them?
- **Would a practitioner in this domain spot something off in five seconds** — the
  specific domain tells in `DOMAINS.md` are the fastest way to find these.

## Comparing multiple options

When the task is "which of these is better" rather than "critique this one thing,"
the same verdict-first shape applies: say which one you'd pick before you explain why.
Then the few reasons that actually drove the decision — not a feature-by-feature
table trying to be exhaustively fair to every option. A real practitioner comparing
two approaches picks one and can tell you the two or three things that made the
difference; that's the target, not a balanced-scorecard writeup.
